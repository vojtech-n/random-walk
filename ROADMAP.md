# Roadmap

Modelled on Cambridge Part IA–IB. Budget: 6–8 h/week, open-ended. Re-plan at each phase boundary.

## Phases

| Phase | Content | Books |
|---|---|---|
| 0 Foundations | Algebra, functions, exp/log, trig, geometry | Lang *Basic Mathematics*. Fallback: AoPS *Precalculus*. Speed drills: Khan Academy |
| 0 Proofs (parallel, ~40% of time) | Logic, sets, direct proof, contrapositive, contradiction, induction, functions, relations | Hammack *Book of Proof* (free) |
| 0→1 Bridge | Properties of numbers, induction | Spivak *Calculus* ch. 1–2, when in hand |
| 1 Part IA-style | Numbers & Sets · Vectors & Matrices · Analysis I · Probability · Groups | Hammack · Strang · Spivak (fallback: Abbott *Understanding Analysis*) · Blitzstein & Hwang · Armstrong *Groups and Symmetry* |
| 2 Part IB-style | Linear Algebra · Analysis II · Markov Chains · Statistics | Axler *LADR* · Abbott/Rudin · Norris · Casella & Berger |
| 3 Applications | ML math, stochastic processes, optimisation | Picked at phase 2 end |

Exercise source for phase 1–2: Cambridge Tripos example sheets (public).

## Method

1. Before reading a proof, cover it. Try it yourself for 10–15 min.
2. Paper first: scratch work, failed attempts, drafts.
3. One clean notebook per concept, in your own words: definition (with Czech term), intuition, your proof, an example and a counterexample. For each hypothesis: drop it, show what breaks.
4. Proof shape: given / to show → technique → proof → check every hypothesis was used.
5. Hand in at supervision. Fix what gets marked.
6. Re-prove from memory after 2 days and after 2 weeks. Known = re-proved cold.
7. Do ≥50% of each chapter's exercises, and every hard one.
8. Anki cards come from your def/thm divs.
9. Python only after a hand attempt, to check or explore.
